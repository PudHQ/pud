# Guerilla Marketing Strategies

Just some random ideas and thoughts about guerrilla-esque ways to market a dev tool...

---

My friend sent me [this video](https://youtu.be/r0lhqUg4O_Y) yesterday. It's the School of Hard Knocks guy, James Dumoulin, which I now generally have a hard time watching knowing that the videos are staged.[^1] In the beginning of this particular video though the interviewee, Howard Panes, talks about growing his electronic cigarette business:

> My secret was going store to store, \[person to person\]
> ...
> I'd spend 20 hours on a line, at 7-Eleven.

Basically saying that he'd spend most of his time talking to potential customers in convenience store queues -- and also talking to the owners and employees of said stores:

> So I went in the stores, asked questions, and said what are your best-selling brands, and what are the challenges you have?
> ...
> The right questions are everything.
> ...
> I mastered guerilla marketing by being in the streets, in the stores, talking to the managers, talking to the workers.

And it got me thinking about something which I often think about: in the _true_ software engineering world -- not SaaS or GTM or finance, but tools for software engineers -- very rarely is intense, clever, guerilla marketing done well or even attempted in the first place. I think the lack of high-octane marketing and sales in developer circles[^2] is partly because in order to market yourself you really do have to put yourself out there. So rarely do I go to a meetup for developers (where fewer sales or business people have been allowed in) and someone actively pitches me their product; God-forbid do they ask me to sign up for it!

[^1]: It's discussed in this (admittedly paywalled, sorry) article here: https://www.businessinsider.com/school-of-hard-knocks-business-lessons-empire-2026-10
[^2]: By "circles" I mean places like meetups, networking events, and hangouts.
